How the U.S.-China Trade War Blew Up: A Timeline

U.S.

CHINA

February 1

Days after taking office, Trump imposes a new 10% tariff on all Chinese imports. That's on top of existing tariffs from his first term and from the Biden era.

February 4

China retaliates with a 15% tariff on U.S. coal and liquefied natural gas, and a 10% tariff on crude oil, agricultural machinery, and large cars.

March 3

Trump raises the U.S. tariff on Chinese imports another 10%. Cumulative new tariff: 20%.

March 4

China counters with a 15% tariff on U.S. chicken, wheat, corn, and cotton, and a 10% tariff on sorghum, soybeans, pork, beef, aquatic products, fruits, vegetables, and dairy products.

April 2

As part of his "Liberation Day" agenda, Trump raises China tariffs another 34%. Cumulative new tariff: 54%.

April 4

China responds with an additional 34% tariff on all U.S. goods, effective April 10.

April 7

Trump retaliates against China's retaliation by imposing another 50% tariff. Cumulative new tariff: 104%. "We are waiting for their call," Trump says on April 8, suggesting he's willing to negotiate with China.

April 9

Countermeasures escalate as China increases its tariffs to 84% on U.S. goods and Trump's 104% duties take effect.

April 9

President Trump announces a 90-day pause on tariffs for many trading partners, while raising tariffs on Chinese imports to 145%.

April 11

China increases its retaliatory tariffs on U.S. imports to 125%.

April 11

The U.S. announces reciprocal tariffs will exclude consumer electronics from tariffs from most countries, but retains a 20% tariff on electronics from China.
